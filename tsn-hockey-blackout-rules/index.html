TSN Hockey Blackout Rules Explained: Channels, Regions & TSN+ (2026–27)

Why regional hockey games get blacked out on TSN feeds, how territory rules divide TSN2, TSN3, TSN4, and TSN5, and why TSN+ will not unlock out-of-market games.

TSN Feeds (TSN1–TSN5) & Cable

Regional Rights Only

Every NHL game shown on TSN is a regional broadcast. TSN holds zero English national rights in Canada (no Saturday night games, no Stanley Cup Playoffs). Broadcast rights are strictly geofenced to individual team markets:

  • TSN2 (Montreal Canadiens): 50 regional games. Unlocked ONLY in Quebec, Atlantic Canada, and Eastern Ontario (east of Belleville).
  • TSN5 (Ottawa Senators): 50 regional games. Unlocked ONLY in Eastern Ontario, Quebec, and Atlantic Canada.
  • TSN4 (Toronto Maple Leafs): 27 regional games. Unlocked ONLY in Ontario west of Belleville. (Sportsnet Ontario carries the other half of Toronto's regional slate).
  • TSN3 (Winnipeg Jets): 50 regional games. Unlocked ONLY in Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Northwest Ontario, Nunavut, and NWT.
  • Out-of-Market Blackout Wall: If you are outside a team's official territory, that TSN regional channel is blacked out on cable and TSN streaming feeds.

The "TSN+" Trap & Out-of-Market Reality

No Out-of-Market NHL

Many cord-cutters subscribe to TSN or the TSN+ streaming add-on hoping to stream their out-of-market team. That setup does not work:

  • TSN+ Has Zero Out-of-Market NHL: TSN+ carries bonus streams for PGA Tour golf, tennis courts, and NCAA sports. It does not carry out-of-market NHL games and does not remove blackouts.
  • TSN Direct Geofencing: Subscribing to TSN directly online checks your device's physical IP address. A subscriber in Toronto gets the exact same TSN2 blackout as a cable customer.
  • The Real Fix (Sportsnet+ Premium): Rogers owns national out-of-market streaming rights in Canada. If you want to watch the Canadiens in Toronto, the Jets in Calgary, or the Sens in Vancouver, you need Sportsnet+ Premium (or NHL Centre Ice), which carries TSN's regional broadcast out-of-market.

The Toronto Habs Fan Dilemma: "I have TSN, so why is the game blacked out?"

It is one of the most common frustrations in Canadian sports: you buy a TSN subscription, see the Montreal Canadiens playing on TSN2, click the channel, and run straight into a blackout screen.

Why does the game appear on your TV guide in the first place? TSN is sold across Canada as a five-feed bundle (TSN1 through TSN5). Because cable providers and the TSN app distribute all five channel guides nationwide, Habs games on TSN2 appear right in your electronic program guide in Toronto, Calgary, or Vancouver. But the moment you click play, postal code and IP geofences verify your location and block the video feed.

Under NHL territorial broadcast bylaws, TSN only purchased the regional rights to show Canadiens games to fans living inside Montreal's home market (Quebec, Atlantic Canada, and Eastern Ontario). When you tune in from Toronto, you are physically inside Maple Leafs territory. TSN is legally prohibited from transmitting the Montreal feed to your screen.

To watch those 50 Habs games in Toronto, you do not need TSN—you need Sportsnet+ Premium (or digital cable NHL Centre Ice). Rogers pays the NHL for national out-of-market redistribution rights. In one of Canadian media's great ironies, Rogers takes Bell's regional TSN2 feed—complete with TSN's own broadcast crew of Bryan Mudryk and Craig Button—and streams it directly through the Sportsnet+ app to out-of-market subscribers.

TSN On-Screen Blackout Error Explained

Troubleshooting
TSN App / TSN.ca Live Video Player Error

"Regional Restriction: This live event is not available in your current area"

The Cause: You are physically outside that team's designated broadcast territory (for example, trying to stream a Montreal Canadiens game on TSN2 from Toronto, or a Winnipeg Jets game on TSN3 from Alberta). Under NHL bylaws, TSN is legally prohibited from broadcasting regional games to out-of-market viewers.

The Fix: Out-of-market regional games cannot be unlocked through a TSN or TSN+ digital subscription. Out-of-market games can only be streamed legally in Canada via Sportsnet+ Premium (which re-distributes TSN's regional feeds nationwide) or through the NHL Centre Ice digital cable package.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "Regional Restriction: This live event is not available in your current area" mean on TSN?

TSN regional broadcasts (Canadiens on TSN2, Jets on TSN3, Leafs on TSN4, Senators on TSN5) are legally geofenced to each club's designated home broadcast territory. If you are physically located outside that territory, the NHL requires TSN to block the stream. Out-of-market games can only be streamed via Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice.

Why is the Montreal Canadiens game blacked out on TSN if I live in Toronto?

TSN only holds regional broadcast rights for the Canadiens on TSN2, which are legally restricted to Quebec, Atlantic Canada, and Eastern Ontario (east of Belleville). Toronto is in the Maple Leafs broadcast zone. Under NHL agreements, TSN must black out TSN2 Canadiens broadcasts to viewers in Toronto and Western Canada. To watch in Toronto, you need Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice.

Does a TSN+ streaming subscription bypass NHL regional blackouts?

No. TSN+ is an add-on streaming product that carries bonus coverage for golf, tennis, and auto racing. It does not carry out-of-market NHL games or remove regional blackouts. A direct TSN digital subscription only provides standard linear TSN feeds, which remain geofenced to each team's local territory.

Does TSN broadcast any national NHL games or Stanley Cup Playoff games?

No. In English, national broadcast rights (including Saturday night Hockey Night in Canada and the entire Stanley Cup Playoffs) belong exclusively to Rogers Sportsnet, while Monday and Wednesday national regular-season games stream on Amazon Prime Video. Every single NHL game shown on TSN is a regional broadcast.

Why are Winnipeg Jets games blacked out on TSN3 in Ontario and Western Canada?

TSN3's regional feed for the Jets is geofenced to Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Northwest Ontario, Nunavut, and the Northwest Territories. Viewers in Ontario (outside Thunder Bay and Kenora), Alberta, BC, Quebec, and Atlantic Canada will find TSN3 blacked out during Jets games and need Sportsnet+ Premium to watch.

Can I watch Toronto Maple Leafs games on TSN4 if I live in Ottawa or Montreal?

No. TSN4 only holds regional rights to 27 Maple Leafs games within Ontario west of Belleville. Viewers in Ottawa, Quebec, and Eastern Canada fall into the Senators and Canadiens broadcast zone, so Leafs games on TSN4 are blacked out. You can watch them using Sportsnet+ Premium.
