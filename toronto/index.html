Toronto Maple Leafs Hockey Broadcast Rights & Regional Blackout Guide (2026–27 Leafs Season)

Following the Toronto Maple Leafs regular season and preseason broadcasts across Canada requires understanding how NHL broadcast territory rights are allocated between national networks and regional partners. For Toronto Maple Leafs fans, games are split across five distinct television and streaming networks: TSN4, , Sportsnet, Amazon Prime Video, and TVA Sports.

Regional Toronto Maple Leafs Games (TSN4 & )

TSN4 (29 English games) hold the official regional rights for the Toronto Maple Leafs. Their broadcast boundary is restricted by NHL carriage rules strictly to Ontario (West of Belleville).

If you reside outside this territory, standard cable subscriptions to TSN4 will show a regional blackout screen during live Toronto Maple Leafs games. Out-of-market Leafs fans must use Sportsnet+ Premium (for online streaming) or add NHL Centre Ice (for traditional cable/satellite TV) to watch regional feeds legally. Note that NHL Centre Ice does not include an online streaming option.

National Leafs Games (Sportsnet, Prime & TVA Sports)

When the Toronto Maple Leafs play on Saturday night Hockey Night in Canada broadcasts, Sportsnet (English) and TVA Sports (French) broadcast coast-to-coast with zero regional blackouts anywhere in Canada. Every Canadian viewer with standard cable or basic streaming packages can watch these matchups.

Select regular-season Toronto Maple Leafs games stream nationally on Wednesday nights exclusively on Amazon Prime Video in English, which also carries coast-to-coast clearance with no regional blackouts.

Quick Summary for Leafs Fans Living Outside the Toronto Region: If tonight's Toronto Maple Leafs game is scheduled on Sportsnet or Prime Video, you can watch anywhere in Canada without blackouts. If the game is on TSN4 or Sportsnet Ontario, you will be blacked out in Eastern Ontario, Quebec, and the rest of Canada unless you have Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice.

Why are Toronto Maple Leafs games blacked out on TSN4 or Sportsnet Ontario in Eastern Ontario?
The NHL broadcast boundary line in Ontario is drawn near Belleville. If you live west of Belleville (including Toronto and the GTA), you are in the Leafs' home territory and can watch regional games on standard cable. If you live strictly in Eastern Ontario (Ottawa, Cornwall, Brockville), you are in the Senators/Canadiens home territory. In that zone, regional Leafs games are blacked out to protect local Sens/Habs rights unless you subscribe to Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice. (Note: The Belleville to Kingston corridor is a designated "Overlap" zone where providers offer feeds for both regions. Use the "Ontario Overlap" region in the dropdown above to accurately see your games).

Why am I getting a Sportsnet blackout or NHL regional blackout tonight?

If your screen goes dark and displays a blackout message on Sportsnet or TSN, the game is a Regional Broadcast and your physical location is outside the team's NHL-defined territory. You must use Sportsnet+ Premium to stream out-of-market regional games.

Even if you pay for the channel through a standard TV package, the NHL forces the network to blackout the signal for out-of-market viewers.

How do I watch Toronto Maple Leafs games in Canada without regional blackouts?

Out-of-market fans living outside the team's regional territory can watch all regional Toronto Maple Leafs games on TSN4 and by subscribing to Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice. This bypasses the standard blackout restrictions for standard cable subscribers.

Are Saturday night Toronto Maple Leafs games blacked out anywhere in Canada?

No. Saturday night Toronto Maple Leafs games on Sportsnet and TVA Sports are part of the NHL's national broadcast package. These games clear coast-to-coast across Canada with zero regional blackouts on standard cable packages.

Which Toronto Maple Leafs games stream exclusively on Amazon Prime Video?

National Wednesday Night Hockey broadcasts featuring the Toronto Maple Leafs are exclusive to Amazon Prime Video in English across Canada. These streams carry no regional blackout restrictions for Prime subscribers anywhere in the country.
