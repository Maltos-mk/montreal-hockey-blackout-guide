Montreal Canadiens Hockey Broadcast Rights & Regional Blackout Guide (2026–27 Habs Season)

Following the Montreal Canadiens regular season and preseason broadcasts across Canada requires understanding how NHL broadcast territory rights are allocated between national networks and regional partners. For Montreal Canadiens fans, games are split across five distinct television and streaming networks: TSN2, RDS, Sportsnet, Amazon Prime Video, and TVA Sports.

Regional Montreal Canadiens Games (TSN2 & RDS)

TSN2 (50 English games) and RDS (45 French games) hold the official regional rights for the Montreal Canadiens. Their broadcast boundary is restricted by NHL carriage rules strictly to QC, Atlantic Canada, Eastern Ontario.

If you reside outside this territory, standard cable subscriptions to TSN2 will show a regional blackout screen during live Montreal Canadiens games. Out-of-market Habs fans must use Sportsnet+ Premium (for online streaming) or add NHL Centre Ice (for traditional cable/satellite TV) to watch regional feeds legally. Note that NHL Centre Ice does not include an online streaming option.

National Habs Games (Sportsnet, Prime & TVA Sports)

When the Montreal Canadiens play on Saturday night Hockey Night in Canada broadcasts, Sportsnet (English) and TVA Sports (French) broadcast coast-to-coast with zero regional blackouts anywhere in Canada. Every Canadian viewer with standard cable or basic streaming packages can watch these matchups.

Select regular-season Montreal Canadiens games stream nationally on Wednesday nights exclusively on Amazon Prime Video in English, which also carries coast-to-coast clearance with no regional blackouts.

Quick Summary for Habs Fans Living Outside Quebec: If tonight's Montreal Canadiens game is scheduled on Sportsnet, Prime Video, or TVA Sports, you can watch anywhere in Canada without blackouts. If the game is on TSN2 or RDS, you will be blacked out in Western Ontario and the West unless you have Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice.

Why are Montreal Canadiens games blacked out on TSN2 in Western Ontario?
The NHL broadcast boundary line in Ontario is drawn near Belleville. If you live strictly in Eastern Ontario (Ottawa, Cornwall, Brockville) or further east into Quebec and Atlantic Canada, you are in the Montreal Canadiens' home territory and can watch regional games on standard cable. However, if you live west of Belleville (including Toronto and the GTA), you are in the Maple Leafs' home territory. In that zone, regional Montreal Canadiens games are blacked out to protect local Leafs rights unless you subscribe to Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice. (Note: The Belleville to Kingston corridor is a designated "Overlap" zone where providers offer feeds for both regions. Use the "Ontario Overlap" region in the dropdown above to accurately see your games).

Why am I getting a Sportsnet blackout or NHL regional blackout tonight?

If your screen goes dark and displays a blackout message on Sportsnet or TSN, the game is a Regional Broadcast and your physical location is outside the team's NHL-defined territory. You must use Sportsnet+ Premium to stream out-of-market regional games.

Even if you pay for the channel through a standard TV package, the NHL forces the network to blackout the signal for out-of-market viewers.

How do I watch Montreal Canadiens games in Canada without regional blackouts?

Out-of-market fans living outside the team's regional territory can watch all regional Montreal Canadiens games on TSN2 and RDS by subscribing to Sportsnet+ Premium or NHL Centre Ice. This bypasses the standard blackout restrictions for standard cable subscribers.

Are Saturday night Montreal Canadiens games blacked out anywhere in Canada?

No. Saturday night Montreal Canadiens games on Sportsnet and TVA Sports are part of the NHL's national broadcast package. These games clear coast-to-coast across Canada with zero regional blackouts on standard cable packages.

Which Montreal Canadiens games stream exclusively on Amazon Prime Video?

National Wednesday Night Hockey broadcasts featuring the Montreal Canadiens are exclusive to Amazon Prime Video in English across Canada. These streams carry no regional blackout restrictions for Prime subscribers anywhere in the country.
