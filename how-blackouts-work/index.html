The Belleville–Kingston Ontario Overlap Zone

In Ontario, the broadcast dividing line between the Toronto Maple Leafs (West) and the Ottawa Senators / Montreal Canadiens (East) runs between Belleville and Kingston.

Wait—what if you live right inside the Belleville-to-Kingston corridor? Cable and streaming providers treat this strip as an official Shared Overlap Zone where viewers are granted in-market access to regional feeds for all three teams (TOR, OTT, and MTL). Our calculator includes a dedicated Ontario Overlap region option to model this exact boundary.

Saskatchewan & Northwestern Ontario Shared Zones

Saskatchewan is a shared three-team territory: viewers receive in-market regional access to the Calgary Flames, Edmonton Oilers (Sportsnet West), and Winnipeg Jets (TSN3).

Northwestern Ontario (Thunder Bay / Kenora) straddles the Manitoba border and is designated as a shared regional zone for both the Winnipeg Jets and Toronto Maple Leafs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do regional hockey blackouts still exist in Canada?

Broadcast rights are split into two separate contracts: National rights (Saturday night Hockey Night in Canada on Sportsnet/TVA Sports and Wednesday/Monday nights on Amazon Prime Video) and Regional rights sold by individual teams to TSN, RDS, or regional Sportsnet feeds. Regional contracts require networks to geofence live games strictly to viewers physically inside that team's local territory.

Where is the Ontario blackout boundary line drawn between Toronto and Ottawa/Montreal?

The dividing line in Ontario runs between Belleville and Kingston. Viewers west of Belleville (including the GTA and Southwestern Ontario) are in the Maple Leafs territory. Viewers east of Kingston (including Ottawa and Cornwall) are in the Senators and Canadiens territory. The corridor between Belleville and Kingston is an official overlap zone with access to both.

Are CBC or Citytv broadcasting free over-the-air hockey games in 2026–27?

Under the 2026–27 broadcast agreements, regular-season games require a cable subscription or streaming service (Sportsnet, TSN, RDS, TVA Sports, or Amazon Prime Video) rather than free over-the-air antenna broadcasts.
